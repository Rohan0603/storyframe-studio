# Narrative quality contract

StoryFrame episodes are narration-led stories, not a sequence of attractive
images. Before a script can be approved, its `story.narrativePlan` must make
the intended dramatic logic explicit:

- `hook`: the first surprising promise to the viewer;
- `contradiction`: the opening mismatch or question that creates curiosity;
- `protagonistGoal`: what a person or group is trying to do;
- `stakes`: what becomes meaningfully worse if that goal fails;
- `escalationBeats`: at least two causal consequences, not two topic headings;
- `reversal`: a decision, discovery or reversal that changes the expected path;
- `endingPayoff`: the final consequence, irony or emotional resolution;
- `voiceDirection`: delivery guidance for pace, emphasis, attitude and pauses;
- `factualGuardrails`: claims, dialogue, motives or numbers the writer must not
  invent or overstate.

These are enforceable minimums, not a formula for every episode. A plan may be
comic, suspenseful, reflective or character-driven, but it must show a causal
chain and a meaningful ending. A historical hook may use a contradiction such
as “a tiny private dispute mobilized national power,” while the script still
has to explain the underlying treaty, people and consequences accurately.

Schema-version-1 episodes without a plan remain readable as legacy artifacts,
but a producer cannot newly approve their script stage until an owner or writer
adds and reviews the plan. If a legacy plan is present, manifest validation
checks its shape; semantic causality and payoff remain explicit human-review
questions below.

## Two-minute review questions

The producer and reviewer should answer these against the actual script and
shot ledger:

1. Does the opening create a specific question or contradiction within the
   first sentence or two?
2. Does each escalation change a character's options, risk or decision?
3. Does the visual action show the consequence being narrated, rather than
   merely restating the topic with a new background?
4. Is there a reversal or comic/ironic turn before the ending?
5. Does the last beat pay off the opening promise and leave a memorable
   consequence?
6. Is the voice direction reflected in the selected offline voice and a human
   preference check, without changing the transcript or inventing performance
   text?

For the Pig War pilot, this review would preserve the excellent potato/pig
premise but ask for a sharper character consequence after the threat of arrest,
an explicit “why is this becoming military?” turn, and a more audible final
reversal: two nations spend years sharing an island because of one dead pig.

## Voice QA, local-only

Kokoro remains the default no-charge path. A future voice comparison should be
bounded to a short, identical script excerpt and locally available voices or
speed settings. Record the voice/model/speed, WAV hash and duration for each
sample, then have a human choose on clarity, energy, warmth, intelligibility
and fatigue. Do not silently replace an approved narration, use a paid
provider, or treat a synthetic loudness/timing metric as proof that a voice is
more engaging.
