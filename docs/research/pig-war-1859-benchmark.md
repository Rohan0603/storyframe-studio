The public synopsis shows a strong causal ladder: ambiguous treaty language →
competing settlers → pig incident → troop and naval escalation → cooler heads →
joint occupation → German arbitration. It also identifies General Harney's
provocative role and Admiral Baynes/Winfield Scott as de-escalating forces.
Those human decisions are important: the story is not “a pig caused a war,” but
“a small incident exposed a live territorial dispute, and ambitious or cautious
people changed what happened next.”

## Historical cross-check

The NPS history page confirms the 1846 treaty's ambiguous “middle of the
channel” wording, the competing Haro and Rosario Straits, the Hudson's Bay
Company farm, Cutlar's June 15, 1859 shooting, Pickett's July 27 landing,
British naval involvement, Baynes's refusal to start a war over the dispute,
joint occupation and the 1872 arbitration result:

- https://www.nps.gov/sajh/learn/historyculture/the-pig-war.htm
- https://www.nps.gov/places/lyman-cutlar-homestead.htm

The NPS homestead account also supports the potato-patch setup and records the
local dispute over compensation and land claims. The pilot correctly avoids
turning contested dollar amounts and reported threats into an uncontested
single-cause fact. The safe narration rule remains: call this a border crisis
or confrontation, not a declared U.S.-British war; no shots were exchanged
between the nations during the crisis; “only casualty was a pig” is the
historical irony, not a claim that the pig itself caused the underlying dispute.

## What worked in the pilot

The 133.650-second, 31-shot pilot has strong visual continuity, a clear
potato/pig hook, source-backed claims, verified alignment and a memorable final
image. Its factual sequence is sound and its images bind to the narration.

## Why it can still feel boring

The script explains the dispute in long informational blocks. Most middle
shots change location or military scale without changing a character's choice.
The pilot names Harney, Pickett, British forces and arbitration, but their
motives and decisions are not used as turns in the story. The ending states the
score, but the narration does not build a sharp expectation that the score will
be absurd until the final line. The result is polished exposition rather than
an escalating chain of consequences.

## Before/after story outline

### Pilot shape

1. Pig in potatoes; the Pig War is introduced.
2. Treaty ambiguity and competing claims are explained.
3. Hudson's Bay Company farm and Cutlar are introduced.
4. Cutlar shoots the pig; compensation dispute follows.
5. American troops arrive; Britain answers with ships and marines.
6. No one fires; joint occupation and arbitration are explained.
7. U.S. wins the boundary; one pig is the only casualty.

### Proposed original shape

1. **Contradiction:** “A pig is eating potatoes—and two countries are about to
   deploy armies.” Promise the viewer the mismatch, not just the topic.
2. **Personal goal:** Cutlar wants his small farm protected; Griffin wants the
   company's claim respected. Their immediate choices make the legal dispute
   human.
3. **Hidden pressure cooker:** reveal the treaty's two channels only after the
   viewer understands why the people already distrust one another.
4. **Causal escalation:** a threat of arrest changes the settlers' options;
   Pickett's landing changes the national stakes; warships change the cost of
   one more mistake.
5. **Reversal:** the expected battle is interrupted by officials who refuse to
   let military momentum decide a local dispute.
6. **Long-tail consequence:** joint occupation lasts twelve years, turning the
   “war” into an uneasy shared routine before arbitration settles the border.
7. **Payoff:** return to the pig/potato image and reveal the absurd ledger:
   years of international tension, zero battlefield deaths, one dead pig.

## Original sample hook

“A British pig walked into an American potato patch. The next thing on San Juan
Island was not a farmer—it was the United States Army. And offshore, British
warships were waiting. The pig did not start this crisis by itself. It wandered
into a border dispute that two countries had been leaving dangerously
unanswered.”

This is an original sample, not copied from the benchmark. It is a direction
for future script work, not a silent replacement of the approved pilot.
